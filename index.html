The 5-step anti-silly-mistake routine

  1. Circle the ask. What exactly must the answer be: x? both x and y? factors?
  2. Copy check. Read the question once more against what you copied. Signs and powers first.
  3. One step per line. Never do two moves in your head. Write the = sign under the = sign.
  4. Sign watch. Moving across = flips the sign. A minus before a bracket flips every sign inside.
  5. Plug it back. Put the answer into the ORIGINAL equation. Both sides equal? Then done.
Step 5 takes about 30 seconds and catches most silly mistakes before the teacher does.

Weak spots get earlier and longer slots: Quadratic Equations (not started in school), Physics Ch 8 (not studied), Valjean (not started in school), Formal Letter and Reading Comprehension (not practised).

What tends to work for a short attention span

  • Short and timed. 25 minutes on, 5 off, using the timer on the Plan tab. Stopping on time builds trust that the session really ends.
  • He teaches you. After a notes card, ask him to explain it to you in 2 minutes. Explaining exposes gaps faster than rereading.
  • Recall before rereading. Flip cards and the quiz first, notes second. Testing yourself beats rereading, so the order matters.
  • Visible wins. Ticking missions off fills the progress bar. Agree a small reward for a fully ticked weekend.
  • Maths: check, not speed. Make "plug it back" a habit in the practice machine. Look at the mistake log together on 30 Nov and 6 Dec.
